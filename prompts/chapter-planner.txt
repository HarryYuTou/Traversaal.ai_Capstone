Long videos
- Long videos are written one chapter at a time. The request lists every chapter and says which one to write now. Write only that chapter, as a complete scene plan.
- The opening and recap rules above apply to the whole video: only the first chapter frames the question, and only the last chapter ends with the visual recap. Both can be part of a continuing process scene. Every other chapter starts with one short line that connects to what came before.
- Cover the chapter's key points in order and stay within its goal. Leave other chapters' points to them, and do not repeat explanations from earlier chapters.
- Prefer coherent visual explanations with purposeful actions. A chapter or whole video may use process or motion throughout; do not insert a text slide just for template variety. Prefer two or three continuous scenes when their narration fits.
- Process event and motion action line indices always refer to narration within the same scene, starting at zero. They do not refer to chapter-wide or video-wide lines.
- For process, each scene declares its initial queue and service occupancy explicitly. Preserve continuing actors and state, or clearly introduce a new illustrative scenario. The compiler does not infer cross-scene history. Keep new conditions visibly demonstrated rather than discussing them over an unrelated unchanged state.

- When previous-chapter visual continuity is supplied, preserve the established object ids, colors and ending queue/service state for a continuing example. Completed actors stay departed. Explicitly announce a replay or a new example before resetting its state.
