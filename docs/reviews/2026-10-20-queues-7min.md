## Findings

### Accuracy: no errors found

Every count in the script is consistent. The sums were checked line by line:

| Time | Claim | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:55 | 3 waiting + 1 served = 4; one leaves → 3; next steps up → 2 waiting, 1 served, still 3 | correct |
| 2:01 | 4 + 2 arrivals − 1 completion = 5 | correct |
| 2:32 | 5 + 1 − 2 = 4 | correct |
| 3:02 | 4 + 1 − 1 = 4 | correct |
| 4:34 | 1 served + 4 waiting = 5; second desk opens → 2 served + 3 waiting, still 5 | correct |
| 4:53 | 5 + 3 − 2 = 6; with one desk 5 + 3 − 1 = 7 | correct |
| 5:29 | 6 − 2 + 1 − 2 = 3 | correct |
| 6:00 | 3 − 1 + 2 = 4; − 1 = 3; + 1 = 4 | correct |

On-screen counts match the narration wherever they are shown. At 0:44 the
counter reads "Waiting 2 · In service 2 · Unfinished 4", at 1:17 it reads
3 · 0 · 3, and at 6:59 it reads 0 · 2 · 2. At 5:18 the picture shows four
waiting and two at desks, and at 5:20 the counter reads 4 · 2 · 6. At 5:15,
before the second arrival walks in, only five people are visible; this is
correct mid-movement, not a missing person.

One simplification is worth a sentence if the script is revised. "Matching
counts hold the backlog steady" (3:22) is true for the exact counts shown. In
real systems with random arrivals, a server that is busy almost all the time
still builds long lines. The video makes no wrong claim, but a viewer could
over-generalise.

### Clarity

The story builds in a sensible order: one desk, then the three counts, then
arrivals minus departures, then timing, then a second desk, then a recap. Each
idea is shown before it is summarised. Possible weak spots to confirm while
watching:

- 0:20, "that customer finishes too": it is not clear who "that customer" is.
- 4:58, "Busy desks are not departures yet." is a hard sentence to hear once.
- Six of the 14 scenes walk through counts step by step (2:01–3:28 and
  4:53–6:36). This is clear, but it may feel repetitive. The video has no
  real-world example beyond a desk, a packing bench and a server.

### Visuals

- **Small, off-centre action.** In every process scene the people, parcels
  and desks sit in the right half of the frame, and the left 40% is empty
  (0:20, 2:20, 3:46, 5:15). On the 720p sharing copy the figures will be small.
- **The counter is often hidden.** The counter is hidden while anything is
  moving, by design. It was visible in only 4 of the 16 frames (0:44, 1:17,
  5:20, 6:59). None of the three parcel-chapter frames (2:20, 2:50, 3:16)
  showed it, and that is where the narration does the most arithmetic. While
  watching, check whether it appears at all between 2:01 and 3:28.
- **4:19, packet beside an idle server.** The narration says "it finishes
  too, and the server sits idle", while a packet still sits just left of the
  server. Check that it is leaving, not waiting.
- **5:47, desk labels between the desks.** "Desk 1" is below the top desk and
  "Desk 2" is above the bottom desk, so both labels sit in the gap. It is
  easy to read them the wrong way round.
- **One scene looks different.** The definition scene (1:32–2:01) uses the
  older slide style, with a blue gradient and large text, between illustrated
  scenes. Harry noted this too.
- **Continuity between scenes.** The desks are labelled "Desk 1 / Desk 2" at
  5:15 and "Desk one / Desk two" at 6:59. Desk 2 changes from teal to dark,
  and parcel tape colours change between scenes. Harry noted the colours.
- No figures overlapped, no text was clipped and no subtitle covered the
  action in any of the 16 frames.
